# Hooks, Pacing, and Retention Techniques

## Hooks

Open with one of: a surprising fact, a painful problem, a contrarian claim, a costly mistake, or an urgent shift. Make the value of the article obvious within the first few lines. Create curiosity by promising a real payoff the article actually delivers.

## Pacing

- Alternate dense explanation with relief: examples, bullets, summaries, or concrete takeaways.
- Break long paragraphs before they feel heavy.
- Move from problem to insight to action instead of staying abstract too long.

## Transitions that pull forward

- End sections with unresolved tension, a new angle, or a practical consequence.
- Use subheads that create forward momentum rather than generic labels.
- Connect each section to the next so the article reads as a guided journey.

## Memorable contrast

- Use before-and-after framing, myth-vs-reality, common mistake vs better approach, or tradeoff comparisons.
- Highlight stakes, consequences, and turning points.
- Surface what is unintuitive, expensive, overlooked, or newly important.

## Scanners and committed readers at once

- Make headlines, subheads, bullets, and summaries meaningful on their own.
- Front-load important ideas in paragraphs and sections.
- A skimming reader should still get the main argument.

## Guardrail

Never buy attention with overpromising, artificial suspense, or drama at the expense of technical truth. The goal is content that is harder to abandon because it is genuinely sharper, clearer, and more rewarding to read - not louder.
